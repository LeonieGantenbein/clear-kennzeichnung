# Examples

Twenty invented examples show what a CLEAR label looks like in very different situations and how to read it. Names and cases are entirely fictitious. The 21st example is real: the label of this collection itself.

## Study, research and teaching

### 1. Master’s thesis: Larissa

Larissa is writing her master’s thesis in history on Italian seasonal workers (*Saisonniers*) in Central Switzerland in the 1960s. She sharpens her research question in conversation with an AI that asks questions and raises objections. The AI finds Italian newspaper articles for her in digitised archives and translates them; Larissa reads and interprets them herself. In the introduction, she has individual phrases smoothed.

```
CLEAR (Larissa): C1, L2, E(p)1, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), DeepL, 02–06/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The line of thought is hers; the AI was a discussion partner (C1). The AI improved the wording but did not intervene in the argument (L2). The sources are published and verifiable by anyone (E(p)1). The thesis thus falls exactly within the [expectation profile](SPECIFICATION.md#reversal-clear-as-an-expectation-profile) that CLEAR proposes for final theses. The label appears next to the declaration of authorship.

### 2. Journal article with own data: Kim

Kim is a postdoc in environmental sciences and spent two years measuring microplastics in four Swiss lakes. During the analysis, the AI suggests explaining the differences between the lakes by the location of the wastewater treatment plants. Kim adopts this interpretation in the discussion. The AI also restructures the discussion and translates the German draft into English.

```
CLEAR (Kim): C1, L(t)3, E(n)4, A: Claude Sonnet 4, Claude Opus 4.1, 05–10/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** E(n)4 is decisive. A conclusion drawn by the AI appears in the article as a result, and it rests on unpublished measurement data that no one outside can check. That is precisely why it must be disclosed. L(t)3 combines both interventions: the AI changed the structure of the discussion (L3) and translated the draft (t). The article’s other contributors declare their shares in lines of their own.

### 3. Lecture notes: Ruth and Andrea

Ruth is a professor of contract law. She revises her lecture notes and has only spelling and punctuation checked. Andrea works as a student assistant at the chair and compiles the case examples. To do so, Andrea has the AI classify thirty decisions of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court: which are landmark decisions and where they contradict each other. Andrea adopts this classification and checks it against five of the decisions.

```
CLEAR (Ruth): C0, L1, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 09/2025, R1
CLEAR (Andrea): C0, L0, E(p)3, A: Claude Sonnet 4.5, 10/2025, R2
```

**Reading:** Two people, two lines. Andrea adopted the AI’s interpretation of the sources (E(p)3) and only spot-checked it (R2). For Ruth, this is a clear signal: before the notes go to the students, she checks the case examples herself or has them checked.

## Work

### 4. Patient leaflet: Merve

Merve is a ward manager in a hospital and writes a leaflet entitled “After knee surgery”. She formulates the content herself; the AI makes the language simpler. The AI then translates the leaflet into Portuguese and Tigrinya. A nurse whose first language is Portuguese checks the Portuguese version in full. An interpreter only spot-checks the Tigrinya version.

German version:

```
CLEAR (Merve): C0, L2, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), 03/2025, R1
```

Portuguese version:

```
CLEAR (Merve): C0, L(t)2, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), DeepL, 03/2025, R1
```

Tigrinya version:

```
CLEAR (Merve): C0, L(t)2, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), 03/2025, R2
```

**Reading:** Each language version is a text of its own and carries its own label. The translated versions carry both interventions in a single statement: the simplified language (L2) and the translation (t), that is, L(t)2. The Tigrinya version is honestly labelled R2. This way, the hospital sees at a glance where a full check is still outstanding.

### 5. Advertising campaign: Nora

Nora is a copywriter at an advertising agency and is working on an autumn campaign for a bakery. She has the AI suggest forty slogans, chooses one and develops it further. She writes the poster texts and social media posts herself; the AI only corrects them.

```
CLEAR (Nora): C2, L1, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 09/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The core idea of the campaign comes from a suggestion by the AI. That is a content part, hence C2, even though everything else is Nora’s. In her own texts, the AI only corrected (L1).

### 6. Product texts in an online shop: Thanh

Thanh runs an online shop for bicycle accessories. For 400 products, Thanh has the AI create descriptions from the manufacturers’ data sheets. Thanh has read forty of them.

```
CLEAR (Thanh): C3, L3, E(p)2, A: Claude Haiku 4.5, 11/2025, R2
```

**Reading:** The texts were comprehensively generated by the AI (C3), namely from its summaries of the data sheets (E(p)2). Whatever the AI overlooked there is also missing from the description. R2 states openly that only samples were checked. Thanh must nonetheless answer for any incorrect statement. The label appears once in the shop’s legal notice and applies to all product texts.

### 7. Client letter at a fiduciary firm: Dragan

Dragan works at a fiduciary firm and informs clients about changed VAT rules. He has the AI summarise the tax administration’s notice and writes the letter on that basis. The AI drafts the section with three calculation examples; Dragan recalculates each one. Finally, the AI smooths the wording.

```
CLEAR (Dragan): C2, L2, E(p)2, A: Gemini 2.5 Pro, 07/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The calculation examples are a content part contributed by the AI (C2). Dragan worked with the summary instead of the notice itself (E(p)2). Because he recalculated every example, he takes full responsibility for the letter (R1).

## Public life and volunteering

### 8. Minutes of the parents’ council: Fatima

Fatima is president of the parents’ council of a primary school. With everyone’s consent, she records the meeting and has the AI produce minutes from the recording. She then compares every item with her notes and corrects two names and one incorrectly recorded decision.

```
CLEAR (Fatima): C3, L3, E(n)2, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), 05/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The AI decided what goes into the minutes and what does not (C3). The basis is an unpublished recording (E(n)2). Because Fatima checked and corrected everything, she takes full responsibility for the minutes (R1). The incorrectly recorded decision shows precisely why R is the decisive statement.

### 9. Local journalism: Gian and Sascha

Gian writes for a local newspaper about the municipal assembly on the renovation of the school building. The AI finds earlier decisions of the municipal council for him, which he reads himself. He has his draft stylistically smoothed. Sascha, the section editor, proofreads the text and has the AI correct it.

```
CLEAR (Gian): C0, L2, E(p)1, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 10/2025, R1
CLEAR (Sascha): C0, L1, E0, A: DeepL Write, 10/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** Two contributors, two lines. There is no legal obligation to label here, because the article has been editorially reviewed and taken responsibility for. CLEAR nonetheless makes visible how it came about, and that strengthens readers’ trust.

### 10. Club newsletter: Daniel

Daniel is the secretary of a gymnastics club and has set things up so that every Friday an AI summarises the announcements from the club chat into a newsletter and sends it out automatically. No one reads it before it goes out.

```
CLEAR (Daniel): C3, L3, E(n)2, A: GPT-4o mini, since 06/2025, R3
```

**Reading:** R3 is uncomfortable but honest. The newsletter goes out unchecked, and members know they should double-check dates if in doubt. Daniel and the club must nonetheless answer for a wrong date. The label appears in the footer of every newsletter.

## School, funding and publishing

### 11. Matura thesis: Lea

Lea is writing her Matura thesis, the final project of Swiss upper secondary school, on wild bees in her neighbourhood. Her school has set out which AI involvement is permitted: at most C1, L1 and E1, plus R1. Lea discusses her research question with an AI and has her handwritten count records transferred into a table. She analysed and interpreted the figures herself. At the end, she has spelling and punctuation corrected.

```
CLEAR (Lea): C1, L1, E(n)1, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), 03–10/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The school reversed CLEAR and used it as an [expectation profile](SPECIFICATION.md#reversal-clear-as-an-expectation-profile). Lea’s label shows at a glance that she kept to it. E(n)1 means: the AI merely made her own unpublished material accessible; Lea interpreted it.

### 12. Research proposal: Samira

Samira is a sociologist and submits a project on commuting behaviour after the pandemic to the Swiss National Science Foundation. The AI analyses published commuter statistics from the Federal Statistical Office and concludes that working from home reduced commuting more in rural areas than in cities. Samira adopts this finding as the starting point of her project. The AI drafts the data management plan on the basis of her project description; Samira checks and supplements it. She has the research plan tightened in structure and argument.

```
CLEAR (Samira): C2, L3, E(p)4, A: Claude Opus 4.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5, 08–10/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** As with [Kim](#2-journal-article-with-own-data-kim), a conclusion drawn by the AI appears as a result (E4). Here, however, it rests on published data (p), and the reviewers can verify it. The AI worked out the data management plan as a content part (C2).

### 13. Novel: Ladina, Beat and Sophie

Ladina is writing a novel about a mountain farming family in Valais. In conversation with the AI, she checks whether her time jumps are coherent, and at the end she has the spelling corrected. Her editor Beat has the AI make stylistic suggestions in tracked-changes mode and accepts some of them. Sophie translates the novel into French, has a first version produced by machine translation and revises every sentence.

```
CLEAR (Ladina): C1, L1, E0, A: Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude Opus 4.1, 01–09/2025, R1
CLEAR (Beat): C0, L2, E0, A: Claude Sonnet 4.5, 10/2025, R1
CLEAR (Sophie): C0, L(t)1, E0, A: DeepL, 11/2025–02/2026, R1
```

**Reading:** Three contributors, three lines: author, editing and translation, exactly as the [specification](SPECIFICATION.md#notation) provides. Each person declares only their own share. The label appears in the imprint. The French edition carries all three lines, the German edition only the first two.

## Administration, law and business

### 14. Municipal press release: Urs

Urs is a municipal clerk. The municipal council has adopted new parking regulations that have not yet been published. Urs has the AI write a press release based on the decision. In doing so, the AI also interprets what will change for residents, and Urs adopts this interpretation. He and the councillor responsible check the release sentence by sentence.

```
CLEAR (Urs): C3, L3, E(n)3, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 09/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The press release informs the public about a matter of public interest. E(n)3 shows that the AI interpreted the consequences of the regulations, and did so from a document the public does not yet know. Because every sentence was checked, Urs stands behind it with R1.

### 15. Legal brief: Blerta

Blerta is a lawyer and drafts an appeal in a building law case. The AI finds relevant decisions of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court for her; Blerta reads each of them herself. She has her draft tightened in structure and argument.

```
CLEAR (Blerta): C0, L3, E(p)1, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 11/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** Invented rulings are a known risk in legal briefs. E(p)1 together with R1 shows: the AI only found the decisions; Blerta read and checked them. The argument is hers (C0), even though the AI rearranged it (L3).

### 16. Job advertisement: Robin

Robin heads the HR department of a mechanical engineering company. For a position in logistics, Robin gives the AI the requirements in bullet points. The AI writes the advertisement from them and adds a section “What we offer”. Robin compares this section with the actual terms of employment and deletes two promises.

```
CLEAR (Robin): C2, L3, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o), 04/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** The requirements come from Robin; the section on benefits was worked out by the AI (C2). That is exactly where the risk lay: Robin would have had to answer for a promise the company does not keep.

### 17. Instagram post: Marc

Marc is a self-employed nutritionist and posts regularly on Instagram. For a post about a new study on coffee and heart health, he has the AI summarise the study and explain what it means for everyday life. He adopts this interpretation in the post. Of the study itself, he has read only the abstract and the conclusion. He has the AI shorten and restructure the text.

```
CLEAR (Marc): C2, L3, E(p)3, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 10/2025, R2
```

**Reading:** On social media, interpretations spread quickly. E(p)3 shows that the interpretation of the study comes from the AI; because the study is published, it can be verified. R2 states honestly that Marc did not check everything. He must nonetheless answer for incorrect health advice. The label appears at the end of the caption.

## Private life

### 18. Cover letter: Sara

Sara is applying for a commercial position at an insurance company. In conversation, the AI asks about her previous experience and helps her work out what she wants to highlight. Sara writes the letter herself and then has it rearranged and shortened.

```
CLEAR (Sara): C1, L3, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-4o mini), 02/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** What the letter says is what Sara experienced and decided; the AI was a discussion partner (C1). The AI changed the structure and length (L3). For a job application, R1 is the most important statement: whoever invites Sara to an interview wants to know that she stands behind every sentence.

### 19. Eulogy: Peter

Peter writes the speech for his father’s funeral service. The memories, anecdotes and thoughts are all his. The AI only helps him shorten the speech to five minutes and rearrange the order of the memories.

```
CLEAR (Peter): C0, L3, E0, A: Claude Sonnet 4.5, 11/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** Even a very personal text can be labelled honestly without losing any of its value. The label is not read aloud. If the speech is printed in the order of service, the label can appear at the end.

### 20. School essay: Noa and Esther

Noa is eleven years old and writes an essay for school about something that happened during the summer holidays. Noa has the AI suggest an opening and several ideas and adopts some of them. Esther, Noa’s mother, reads the essay through, has the spelling corrected with the help of AI and discusses the changes with Noa.

```
CLEAR (Noa): C2, L0, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 08/2025, R1 (Esther)
CLEAR (Esther): C0, L1, E0, A: ChatGPT (GPT-5), 08/2025, R1
```

**Reading:** At eleven, Noa can make the statements but cannot yet take responsibility for the text. R1 (Esther) means: the text has been fully checked, and Esther takes responsibility for it on Noa’s behalf. In her own line, she also declares how she herself worked with the AI.

> **For discussion:** Version 1.0 does not yet provide for responsibility taken on someone else’s behalf, with a name in brackets. It is also an open question from what age a child can set R themselves.

## This collection

### 21. The examples themselves: Leonie

Leonie developed CLEAR and wanted examples from as many areas of life as possible. She set the categories, had names and models corrected and contributed cases of her own, such as the child for whom the mother takes responsibility. The cases themselves, the labels and the readings were invented and written by the AI on the basis of Leonie’s specification. Leonie checked them in the conversation and had them revised several times.

```
CLEAR (Leonie Gantenbein): C3, L3, E0, A: Claude Opus 5.5, 10/2026, R1
```

**Reading:** The content was comprehensively generated by the AI (C3), even though the framework and individual ideas come from Leonie. The wording and structure also come from the AI (L3). E0 is given because the examples rest on Leonie’s own specification; the AI did not stand between her and third-party material. Which models were available when, however, comes from the AI’s knowledge and not from a source. The AI itself proposed the label, as the prompt in the [factsheet](FACTSHEET.en.md#prompt-for-a-language-models-self-declaration) provides. Leonie set R1 herself after checking everything, because only the human can make this statement.
