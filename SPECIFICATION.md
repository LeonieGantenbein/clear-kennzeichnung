# Specification v1.0

## Starting point

Technical watermarks will not create transparency for texts. A watermark only answers whether an AI model was involved – not in what way. It says nothing about the substance, the extent of the involvement or who stands behind the text. Where a human wrote the text and an AI merely smoothed it, it does not apply at all. Nor does the EU AI Act require differentiated information; it requires labelling only in a few narrowly defined cases.

Where obligation and technology fall short, voluntary disclosure remains. CLEAR consists of five statements per person involved.

## The five axes

### C – Conception

- **C0** line of thought developed purely by humans
- **C1** AI as a discussion partner during conception
- **C2** individual content parts worked out by AI
- **C3** content comprehensively generated by AI

### L – Language (linguistic editing)

- **L0** language formulated purely by humans, without AI
- **L1** proofreading by AI
- **L2** stylistic editing by AI: linguistic improvement of the wording without changing the content
- **L3** substantive editing by AI, with interventions in structure and argument
- **L4** translation by AI

### E – Evidence

The sources on which the text rests. The levels indicate how far the AI stood between the human and the material.

- **E0** source access and processing without AI
- **E1** source access: the AI found, obtained or translated sources; the human read and interpreted them
- **E2** source processing: the AI read and summarised, and work continued with these summaries instead of the primary source – whatever is missing there is also missing in the text
- **E3** source interpretation: the AI interpreted what the sources say, what is sound and what contradicts what, and this interpretation was adopted
- **E4** interpretation of results: the AI drew a conclusion from the results, and this conclusion appears in the text as a result

Suffix: **(p)** published material · **(n)** own, unpublished material.

It indicates whether readers can verify what happened. With (p), one can go to the source; with (n), one cannot. E(p)4 will come to light sooner or later; E(n)4 – an interpretation of one’s own data proposed by the AI – never will. That is precisely why it must be disclosed.

### A – Attribution

AI models used and the time period. Anyone reading the text in ten years’ time will thus know which state of the tools they are dealing with.

### R – Responsibility

- **R1** fully checked and taken responsibility for by the human
- **R2** spot-checked and therefore not fully the human’s responsibility
- **R3** published unchecked and therefore no one’s responsibility

## Notation

```
CLEAR (role): C?, L?, E(p|n)?, A: models, period, R?
```

- Only the highest level reached per axis; it includes the levels below.
- Each person involved declares their own share and takes responsibility for it. Several people involved (author, editor, translator …) each get a line of their own.
- The label appears before or after the text.

## Example

```
CLEAR (author): C1, L3, E(p)1, A: Claude Opus 4.8, Fable 5, Opus 5, 04–09/2026, R1
```

Read as: The line of thought is the author’s own; the AI was a sparring partner – it contradicted, asked questions, raised objections, but did not dictate the position (C1). In the writing, its involvement went beyond smoothing: structure, rearranging arguments, transitions (L3). The AI looked up references; all sources are published and verifiable (E(p)1). Every passage was checked and is taken responsibility for (R1). Several models, because the text was written over several months.

## Classification guidance

The boundary between L2 and L3 is the boundary between wording and argument. Anyone reading a revised text as a whole sees only the result and can no longer tell what came from them. The feeling that everything was merely smoothed systematically leads to too low a classification.

Practical advice: always have linguistic revisions by AI made in tracked-changes mode (Word: Track Changes; Google Docs: Suggesting mode; plain text: comparing versions) and go through the suggestions one by one. This instruction can be included in the prompt.

- If there are changes that merge sentences, swap the order or tighten an argument, it is L3 – regardless of how many purely stylistic corrections stand alongside them. The axis measures the deepest involvement, not the most frequent.
- Anyone who has confirmed every change individually has actually checked the text: R1 remains achievable.

## Reversal: CLEAR as an expectation profile

CLEAR is first of all a self-declaration, but it can be reversed: those who receive texts can use the same statements to say what they expect. For schools, upper secondary schools and universities, this would be a language that has so far been missing. Example for final theses:

| Axis | Requirement | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| R | R1 mandatory | A kind of signature; the only level compatible with an examination performance |
| L | up to L2, in writing-intensive subjects possibly up to L3 | depending on the subject |
| C | at most C1 | The line of thought is precisely the achievement being assessed |
| E | E1 | Source criticism is the craft a thesis must demonstrate |

An institution that sets this down does not ban tools, but names levels and teaches how to deal with them.

## Other media (open)

CLEAR is designed for texts. Images, moving images and code need an adapted form. The basic question remains: In what respects was the AI involved, and who stands behind it?

- A and R can be adopted unchanged.
- Image/video: design takes the place of language (retouching, additions, generation of entire image parts). For documentary material, E becomes the question of whether what is shown actually took place.
- Code: implementation takes the place of language. E asks about adopted libraries and code bases, R about tests and review.

How the levels should look in detail is best worked out by those who work with these media. Suggestions are welcome.

## Limits

CLEAR has not yet been tested or evaluated in practice and is certainly incomplete. The label proves nothing; it remains a self-declaration. A precondition for its effectiveness is a culture in which disclosure is not penalised → chapter [“Culture before Labelling”](CULTURE.md).
